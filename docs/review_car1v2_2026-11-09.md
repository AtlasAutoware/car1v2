# Car 1 v2: independent review, 2026-11-09

Scope: the power board schematic as car 1 v2 will use it, the BOM math in `bom/car1v2/`, and the budget.
Read-only. This file is the only thing I wrote. Nothing was ordered, committed or pushed.

A commit landed while I was reading (ffbe478, 21:46 UTC). It changed `docs/CAR1V2.md` and
`docs/CAR1V2_CHECKLIST.md` and added `docs/CAR1V2_BUILD.md`. I read those again. Line numbers below are
for ffbe478.

## 1. Verdict

- **Schematic: pass with fixes.** The E-stop path is hardware, as described. One part value on the dev-kit
  feed (R419) is likely to keep the Jetson unpowered. One wire (Pico GP10 on the E-stop node) lets firmware
  defeat the loop. Neither needs new copper for stage 1.
- **BOM math: pass with fixes.** Every total reproduces to the cent. Two substitute capacitor lines in the
  DigiKey cart are the wrong can and must not be bought. The DigiKey tariff is about $2 low.
- **Budget: pass with fixes.** The arithmetic is right. Stage 1 stays under $1,500 in a pessimistic case
  ($1,225). Stage 1 plus phase 2 does not. Several things the build needs are in no cart.

"Blocker" below means: fix before that order or that build step. It does not mean redesign.

## 3. Detailed answers

### How I checked the schematic

I read `design.py` and `chips_power.py`, then checked them against three other things. First, the netlist in
`atlas_power_parts.json` (215 parts, 133 nets). Second, the net labels in the `.kicad_sch` sheets for the
nets that matter here. Third, the pad nets in `atlas_power.kicad_pcb`: all 574 pads match the netlist. The
sheets are KiCad 9 format files (`version 20250114`), not KiCad 7. The board file is KiCad 7 format.

### 1a. E-stop

The path, in order:

1. `+3V3` (the Pico's 3.3 V output) through R1001 (1 kOhm) to J1001 pin 1, net ESTOP_SRC.
2. The normally closed button and its two wires.
3. J1001 pin 2, net ESTOP_IN, with BAT85 clamps D1001 to GND and D1002 to +3V3.
4. R1002 (100 Ohm) to the node ESTOP_OK, which has R1003 (10 kOhm) and C1001 (10 nF) to GND.
5. ESTOP_OK to U302 (74HCT08) pins 2 and 5. Pin 1 is MAIN_EN, pin 4 is PRECHARGE_EN.
6. U302 pin 3, AND_MAIN, through R305 (1 kOhm) to MIC_IN, with R306 (100 kOhm) to GND.
7. U301 (MIC5014) pin 2 in, pin 5 out to SW_GATE, then R301 to R303 (10 Ohm) to the three gates. R304
   (2.2 MOhm) ties gate to source.
8. For precharge: U302 pin 6, AND_PRE, through R311 to Q305, which pulls the gate of Q304.

There is no firmware-controlled part in series. The cases asked about:

- **Loop wire broken or button unplugged.** ESTOP_IN floats. R1003 holds ESTOP_OK at 0 V. Both AND outputs
  go low. R306 holds MIC_IN low. The switch opens and precharge is off. The node falls below the gate's 0.8 V
  low level in about 0.13 ms.
- **Pico unpowered or pulled out.** `+3V3` has no other source, so the loop has no supply and ESTOP_OK is 0 V.
  R307 and R308 (100 kOhm) hold MAIN_EN and PRECHARGE_EN low. The switch opens. The Jetson's feed also drops
  (finding S4).
- **Pico in reset, pins floating.** R307 and R308 hold both enables low. The switch opens. ESTOP_OK does not
  depend on the Pico's pin state in reset.
- **74HCT08 out of its socket or without 5 V.** R306 holds MIC_IN low. Switch open.
- **MIC5014 out of its socket.** R304 holds the gates at the source. Switch open.

What is not as strong as the docs say:

- GP10 sits directly on ESTOP_OK (finding S2). The shipped firmware makes it an input with no pull
  (`pins.py`:65, `hal.py`:52-53) and hangs an interrupt on it (`hal.py`:112). No code path makes it an output.
  But a wrong pin table, a bug, or someone at the REPL can drive it high, and 10 kOhm cannot hold it down. An
  internal pull-up alone cannot defeat the loop: I estimate under 0.6 V at the node. I took the RP2040's
  pull-up value (50 to 80 kOhm) from memory. Not verified.
- The loop is one channel. A short between its two wires reads as "closed". So does pin 2 touching anything
  above 2 V.
- A main FET that fails shorted defeats the hardware layer. Rev B answered that with "the Pico can tell the
  BMS to open" (`REV_B.md`:472-473). On car 1 v2 the BMS is not on the UART, so that answer is gone (S7).
- The E-stop cuts the motor bus only. The UBEC is on that bus, so steering drops too and the car coasts.
  The Jetson stays on. This is by design, and worth saying to the students.

separate branch into BAT and is not in the way.

Two inputs change behaviour against rev B: the BMS never answers, and the lidar feed stays off. The firmware
handles both.

**The over-voltage cut.** With R416 100 kOhm, R417 15.0 kOhm, R418 1.2 MOhm and the gate at 0.405 of the feed:

- Trip: 18.7 V with no reference current, 18.9 V with the typical 2 uA. With 1 % resistors, the A-grade
  reference (2.470 to 2.520 V) and 0 to 4 uA, the range is about 18.2 to 19.6 V. Allowing for drift, about 18.0 V
  at the lowest. The docs say 18.7 V.
- Release: 17.85 V nominal, 17.4 V at the low corner, about 17.2 V with drift. The docs say 17.85 V.
- A full pack at 16.8 V is at least 1.2 V under the lowest trip and at least 0.4 V under the lowest release.
  The highest trip, 19.6 V, is under the carrier's 20 V. So the thresholds are right.
- The problem is not the threshold. It is the off-state current (S1). The TI datasheet gives the off-state
  cathode current only for Vref = 0, and lists 2 uA typical into REF and 0.4 mA typical minimum cathode
  current. The REF pin here sits at 1.6 V (12 V pack) to 2.24 V (16.8 V pack). In that state the Wikipedia
  article on the TL431 puts the cathode current at 100 to 200 uA, climbing to 300 to 500 uA close to the
  threshold. A TI application note (SNVAA58) describes the same state as a few microamps, so the sources
  disagree. At 100 uA, R419 would drop 1 V, which is more than Q404 needs to turn on. Q404 then holds Q402
  off, and R418 feeds REF a little more, so it stays that way. `netcheck.py` cannot see this because it
  models the off state as an open circuit. With 470 Ohm at R419, Q404 needs about 1.2 mA to turn on, which
  is above the 0.6 mA maximum the datasheet gives for the A grade, and it still gets 2 mA of base current
  at the trip point. The build guide's test 2 would catch this on the bench. The fix costs nothing.

**The ADC dividers.** At 16.8 V the pins see 2.56 V. They reach 3.3 V at 21.6 V on the bus. At the pack TVS
clamp (27.7 V) the divider would give 4.2 V open-circuit, so the Pico's pad diode conducts: about 30 uA. At
the motor-bus clamp (30.6 V), about 60 uA. So they do not stay under 3.3 V at the clamp. They rely on the
pad diode at a very small current for the length of the spike. I did not read the RP2040's limits. Not
verified.

**Regen.** The repo's answer is the VESC's 18 V input limit, with the TVS parts as backstop. That is unchanged.
The missing piece for this car is the pair of regen settings in S5.

### 1d. Supervisor with no BMS traffic

- `python3 -m pytest firmware/supervisor/tests -q`: 126 passed. The Jetson tools: 77 passed. Both match
  `CAR1V2.md`:142-143.
- Boot and main switch with a silent BMS: yes. `main.App` always builds a `JbdClient`; it times out and
  reports offline; `_bms_watch` does nothing more (`sequencer.py`:468-479). The rig reaches RUN with MAIN_EN
  and VESC_KILL high and the lidar feed off.
- Low pack from its own ADC: yes. `_battery` (`sequencer.py`:431-466) uses the filtered BAT_SENSE reading only.
- Thresholds: `car_profile.py`:20-21 has 14.0 V and 13.2 V. `BAT_LOW_MS` 30 s and `BAT_CUT_MS` 10 s are
  inherited. These match `CAR1V2.md`:69-70 and the VESC cut-off line.
- I ran the rig at the edges, outside the repo. 14.05 V: no event in 60 s. 13.95 V: warning at 30.1 s. 13.25 V:
  warning only. 13.15 V: shutdown requested at 10.1 s. A sag from 15.2 V to 12.9 V needs about 14 s before it
  cuts, because of the 2 s filter. A pack that reads under 10 V at power-on ends in FAULT.
- Sensible for a 4S LiPo? Yes as starting values. 3.50 V a cell at cruise current is roughly the last fifth
  of the pack. 3.30 V held for 10 s leaves room above 3.0 V. Two caveats: S3 (the Jetson acts at 14.0 V on its
  own, so the 13.2 V step is rarely reached) and S6 (measurement error against the BMS's 3.20 V).
- Test quality: F1.
- One thing outside the supervisor: the Jetson's estimator uses 8.4 Ah (`board.py`:51) and counts charge
  from BMS current. With no current, its state of charge stays at the start value. "Battery telemetry, pack
  level" means voltage only.

### 1e. The servo

- The UBEC's input is J401 (BEC+, net VM) and J402 (GND). Its output plugs into J403: pin 1 not connected,
  pin 2 SERVO_V, pin 3 GND. J404 and J405 are solder pads on the same nets.
- The servo plugs into J406: pin 1 SERVO_SIG, pin 2 SERVO_V, pin 3 GND.
- The FSESC's signal comes in on J904 pin 1 through R903 (10 kOhm) to U302 pin 9. The buffer output goes
  through R410 (220 Ohm) to J406 pin 1. J904 pin 2 is not connected, so the FSESC's own 5 V cannot meet the
  UBEC's 7.4 V. J904's ground goes through R906 (3.3 Ohm).
- Parts on SERVO_V: J403, J404, J406, C404 (1000 uF 16 V) and TP403. Nothing rated 5 V is on it. The only
  5 V part near it is the buffer, and it drives the signal pin through 220 Ohm. 7.4 V is safe. 8.4 V would be
  too.
- Copper: the class is 2.5 mm on 2 oz. The built track is 2.0 mm for 61 mm, 1.9 mm for 27 mm and 1.6 mm for
  9 mm (`LAYOUT_NOTES.md`:300). That carries several amps.
- There is no fuse or polyfuse on SERVO_V, and none on the UBEC's input lead (S8). The 2.54 mm header pins
  are rated about 3 A (from memory, not read today) and a stalled 35 kg cm servo can draw more for a moment. If the UBEC has bare output
  wires, solder them to J404 and J405 instead of using J403. I did not find the INJS235's stall current. Not
  verified.
- The substitute bought for C404 has the wrong lead spacing (B1).
- The UBEC loads the motor bus during precharge. Checklist C2 and build test 4 cover that.

### 1g. Other defects, ranked

1. S1, the TL431 off-state current. Likely to stop the Jetson powering at all.
2. S2, GP10 on the E-stop node.
3. S3, the Jetson shutting down at the warning level.
4. S4, the Pico-pull test cutting the Jetson.
5. S5 and S6, the missing regen settings and the uncalibrated cut level.
6. S7 to S11, notes.

Two more small ones. There is no reverse-polarity protection at the BAT pads, and D101 conducts forward if the
pigtail is soldered backwards, so meter the pigtail before the first plug-in. And the BOM names TL431ACLPRE3,
a formed-lead part, for a straight-lead TO-92 footprint; the by-hand line allows TL431ACLP, which is the
straight one.
